About

I am a final-year B.Tech. student in Computer Science and Engineering at Ahmedabad University, graduating in May 2027. My research interests are probabilistic inference, wireless network scheduling and high-performance Bayesian computation.

Over the past two years I have worked in three quite different settings. At the MICxN research group I study deadline-aware scheduling for IEEE 802.11bn (Wi-Fi 8) networks. At the Physical Research Laboratory I built parallel Bayesian sampling workflows for pulsar timing array searches. Earlier, at a chemical research laboratory, I modelled fluid flow in laminar jet loop reactors. A paper from each of these areas is now published or under review.

I enjoy the engineering side of research as much as the mathematics: writing the simulator, profiling the sampler, and building the tooling that lets a team reproduce a result.

Away from the screen I read and write. I keep notebooks of poems and short memoir pieces, and I read steadily, mostly books about how people work, decide and build things. Books on productivity and habits have shaped how I organise my own days, and biographies of entrepreneurs have taught me a good deal about independent thinking and staying with a hard problem.

My education has been deliberately broad. I have taken coursework in operating systems, wireless communication and graphical models, and worked on projects in fluid dynamics and astrophysics. I value that mix because the most useful ideas I have found tend to travel between fields.

I also serve as Head of the Student Placement Committee and as Head of Curation for TEDxPrahladnagar at my university.

Selected research work

Wi-Fi 8 multi-AP scheduling

MICxN, advised by Prof. Dhaval Patel. May 2025 to present

I built MATLAB simulations of IEEE 802.11bn multi-AP downlink networks with coordinated spatial reuse, under bursty traffic at medium and high load. The scheduler is earliest-deadline-first, with group selection based on Lyapunov drift and a Robbins-Monro stochastic approximation step, and comes with convergence bounds. Across 16 stations it lowered the 99th-percentile delay against three baselines.

MNP: maximum number of packets. OP: oldest packet. TAT: traffic alignment tracker.

Pulsar timing array analysis on HPC

Physical Research Laboratory, mentored by Mr. Churchil Dwivedi. May to July 2026

Pulsar timing arrays search for a gravitational-wave background by looking for correlated timing residuals across many pulsars. I built CPU-parallel sampling workflows with MPI and SLURM, benchmarked pocoMC, PTMCMCSampler and parallel_bilby on simulated data, and deployed a model-comparison pipeline for Hellings-Downs and Common Red Noise Bayes factors on 10 and 15-year datasets.

The Hellings-Downs curve: the expected correlation between pulsar pairs produced by an isotropic gravitational-wave background.

Beyond the Lab

Reading is the habit I would least like to give up. I tend to alternate between two kinds of books. The first kind is practical self-help, which I read for method. Who Moved My Cheese? is a short parable about adapting when circumstances change, and I return to it whenever a plan falls apart. Getting Things Done gave me a workable system for capturing commitments so that my attention is free for the work itself. Deep Work convinced me to protect long, uninterrupted blocks of time, which is a large part of how research gets finished.

The second kind is books by and about entrepreneurs. I read them less for business advice than for how their authors think: how they form a view of their own, hold to it under pressure, and decide what to ignore.

I also write. I keep poems and memoir pieces, mostly for myself, and writing is how I work out what I actually think about something. I believe it has made my technical writing clearer, since a paper or a README has to hold a reader's attention in much the same way.

What connects all of this for me is independent thinking: reading widely, questioning the default approach, and being willing to work out a problem from first principles before reaching for the standard tool.
